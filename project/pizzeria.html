Pizzeria

What defines a Pizza shop in Argentina?

These are a few of the stories you will tell how it is

In Argentina, a pizzeria is more than just a place to eat; it's a social club. The walls are often covered in black and white photos of famous visitors, tango singers, and football stars. The waiters, known as "mozos", have been working there for decades and know the regulars by name.

The pizza itself is unique: thicker dough ("media masa"), an abundance of cheese that spills over the edges, and the ritual of eating it standing up at the counter ("al corte") with a glass of moscato wine. It is a tradition that has been passed down through generations of Italian immigrants and adapted to the local palate.

Every neighborhood has its own legendary spot, its own secret recipe for "fainá" (chickpea pancake), and its own stories of late-night gatherings after the theater.
